## Why this control matters

Access Request Routing is a record, not a reminder to be careful. It shows what the team checked, who owned the next step, and what remained open before the client acted. The control matters because a broad role is granted because the request had no scope. For the help desk ticket, the analyst records the system, the user impact, the evidence source, and the named owner, and checks the same record with the shift lead before the next customer update.

## Open the governing record

Start with the access request and approval. Record the source, the version or date observed, the owner, and the field the evidence supports. Save the original rather than retyping it into a summary. The assistant prepares and checks the record; the account owner keeps the decision that changes a client commitment, a payment, a schedule, or access. ## Where the handoff breaks

The predictable failure is this: a broad role is granted because the request had no scope. It becomes expensive when the next owner cannot tell whether the item is a request, an approval, or a correction. Use one status set: ready, exception, approved, and closed. A status without an owner and a date is only a label. ## A worked outsourced example

A user asks for reporting access. The record names read-only scope, the approver, and the expiry review. The point is not the specific number. ## Weekly review and stop rule

Sample one ordinary item, one exception, and one recently corrected item. Track completeness and the age of what remains open. Count errors by rule rather than by person.
